   169→   169→## 7. Core Product Principles
   170→   170→
   171→   171→All decisions must follow these principles:
   172→   172→
   173→   173→1. **Security by default**
   174→   174→   - deny by default
   175→   175→   - least privilege
   176→   176→   - minimal exposed surface area
   177→   177→   - secure failure modes
   178→   178→
   179→   179→2. **Auditability above convenience**
   180→   180→   - every sensitive action must be traceable
   181→   181→   - no silent mutations
   182→   182→   - no irreversible actions without proof
   183→   183→
   184→   184→3. **Data integrity first**
   185→   185→   - document content, metadata, and audit records must be protected against tampering
   186→   186→   - version history must be trustworthy
   187→   187→
   188→   188→4. **Privacy by design**
   189→   189→   - minimize unnecessary data collection
   190→   190→   - protect personal data
   191→   191→   - support lawful retention and deletion
   192→   192→
   193→   193→5. **Operational resilience**
   194→   194→   - backups
   195→   195→   - restore capability
   196→   196→   - monitoring
   197→   197→   - incident readiness
   198→   198→
   199→   199→6. **Human-in-the-loop governance**
   200→   200→   - AI may assist, classify, summarize, and suggest
   201→   201→   - AI must not silently make high-risk governance decisions without human approval
   202→   202→
   203→   203→7. **No misleading claims**
   204→   204→   - do not claim certification, accreditation, or compliance status unless actually achieved
   205→   205→   - use phrases such as “designed to support”, “aligned with”, or “target readiness”
   206→   206→
   207→   207→8. **Localization parity**
   208→   208→   - Arabic and English must have equal product quality
   209→   209→   - RTL must be treated as a first-class layout mode, not a mirrored afterthought
   210→   210→
   211→   211→9. **Build for verification**
   212→   212→   - every major feature must include tests, logs, metrics, documentation, and localization checks

   842→   842→## 10. Mandatory Security Requirements
   843→   843→
   844→   844→Smart EDMS must follow a high-assurance security baseline.
   845→   845→
   846→   846→### 10.1 Zero Trust Principles
   847→   847→
   848→   848→Design assumptions:
   849→   849→
   850→   850→- network is not inherently trusted
   851→   851→- users and devices must be continuously verified
   852→   852→- every request must be authenticated and authorized
   853→   853→- least privilege is enforced by default
   854→   854→- sessions and tokens are short-lived where practical
   855→   855→- privileged access is tightly controlled
   856→   856→

   858→   858→
   859→   859→### 10.2 Authentication Requirements
   860→   860→
   861→   861→- enforce strong password policy if passwords are used
   862→   862→- support MFA
   863→   863→- support TOTP and preferably WebAuthn/passkeys
   864→   864→- enforce lockout or rate-limiting against brute force
   865→   865→- support session timeout
   866→   866→- support concurrent session controls
   867→   867→- require re-authentication or step-up for sensitive actions
   868→   868→- service accounts must have narrow scopes and no interactive login unless necessary
   869→   869→
   870→   870→Approved libraries:
   871→   871→
   872→   872→- `argon2`

   889→   889→

   894→   894→Context to consider:
   895→   895→
   896→   896→- tenant
   897→   897→- user role
   898→   898→- group membership

   902→   902→- legal hold state
   903→   903→- retention state
   904→   904→- device trust
   905→   905→- IP risk
